Tuesday, 6 October — Arrival and an easy Eixample evening

Your flight arrives late enough that this should remain a recovery evening, with no ticketed sightseeing.

  • 16:05: Scheduled arrival of easyJet U2 2001 at Barcelona–El Prat Airport. The time is local Barcelona time. Check the easyJet app shortly before travel for the confirmed terminal; easyJet commonly uses Terminal 2, but terminal assignments can change.
  • 16:05–17:15: Allow approximately 45–70 minutes to disembark, complete any required entry formalities, and collect luggage.
  • 17:15–18:00: Take a licensed taxi from the official rank to Mihlton Barcelona Boutique B&B. The drive is usually about 30–45 minutes but can take longer in rush-hour traffic.
  • Approximately 18:00: Check in and rest. If the flight or baggage is delayed, go directly to dinner after settling in.
  • 19:30–20:30: Have dinner near the hotel. Convenient areas include Carrer d’Enric Granados, Rambla de Catalunya, and the streets around Provença and Mallorca.
  • After dinner, optional: Take a gentle 15–30 minute stroll along Rambla de Catalunya or see the illuminated exterior of La Pedrera, which is only a few minutes from the B&B. Your departure schedule leaves no sightseeing time on Sunday, so do this tonight only if you still feel comfortable.

Easy dinner ideas: Bodega Joan for Catalan comfort food, La Pepita for lively tapas, or a restaurant along Enric Granados. Check current Tuesday hours and reserve if possible.

If the flight is delayed: Skip the walk. Check in, eat close to the B&B, and start sightseeing fresh on Wednesday morning.

Wednesday, 7 October — Gaudí and Modernisme

Morning

  • 08:45: Leave the hotel. Take metro L5 from Diagonal to Sagrada Família; allow about 25 minutes door to door.
  • 09:30–11:15: Sagrada Família. Book a timed entry and arrive about 15 minutes early. The official October schedule currently lists Monday–Friday opening from 09:00 to 19:00, but the basilica notes that schedules can change for special events. See the official schedule and buy only through the official ticket page.
  • A tower is optional. Skip it if either of you dislikes heights, enclosed spaces, or narrow stairs; the basilica interior and museum already make a complete visit.
  • 11:15: Walk along the pedestrian-friendly Avinguda de Gaudí.
  • 11:45–13:00: Recinte Modernista de Sant Pau. This colorful Art Nouveau complex complements Sagrada Família and is usually calmer.

Afternoon and evening

  • 13:15–14:30: Lunch along Avinguda de Gaudí or near Sant Pau.
  • 14:30–16:30: Return to the B&B by L5 and rest.
  • 17:00–18:30: Casa Batlló. It is about a 15-minute walk from the B&B. The standard visit takes approximately 75 minutes. The official Casa Batlló site lists timed visits and current experiences.
  • 19:00: Easy stroll back along Passeig de Gràcia.
  • 20:00: Dinner in Eixample.

Lower-energy version: Visit Sagrada Família, have lunch, and return to the hotel. Keep Casa Batlló; omit Sant Pau.

Thursday, 8 October — Palau de la Música, Gothic Quarter and El Born

Wear shoes with good grip: the Old City has cobbles and uneven paving.

Morning

  • 08:50: Take L3 from Diagonal to Catalunya, then walk to the Palau.
  • 09:30–10:25: Palau de la Música Catalana. Choose a guided or audio-guided architectural visit. Check the official Palau calendar before booking; if an appealing concert is offered during your stay, consider returning one evening instead.
  • 10:30–12:30: Gothic Quarter walk:
    1. Plaça Nova and the Roman wall
    2. Barcelona Cathedral and cloister
    3. Carrer del Bisbe
    4. Plaça Sant Jaume
    5. Temple of Augustus columns
    6. Plaça del Rei

Do not rush to cover every lane; the pleasure is in the architecture and small squares.

Afternoon and evening

  • 12:45–14:15: Lunch in the Gothic Quarter or El Born.
  • 14:15–16:30: El Born: Visit Santa Maria del Mar, then choose one of:
    • Picasso Museum — best for art lovers; reserve a timed ticket.
    • El Born Centre de Cultura i Memòria plus a slower neighborhood stroll — less tiring.
  • 16:30: Coffee, then walk through Parc de la Ciutadella or continue to Port Vell if you still have energy.
  • 17:30–18:00: Taxi back to the B&B for a rest.
  • 20:00: Dinner near the hotel, or return to El Born only if you have a concert reservation.

Food ideas in El Born: Bar del Pla, Tapeo, or Cal Pep. These can be busy, so reserve where accepted or arrive early.

Rain option: Extend the Palau visit, visit the Picasso Museum, and use taxis between the hotel and Old City.

Friday, 9 October — Park Güell and Gràcia

Morning

  • 08:50: Take a taxi from the B&B to the Carretera del Carmel entrance. This starts you near the upper side and reduces uphill walking.
  • 09:30–11:30: Park Güell. Reserve the 09:30 entry. Morning is cooler and generally more comfortable. The official site currently lists timed access and warns that capacity is limited; use the official prices and times page. Your ticket is tied to its stated entry window.
  • Follow a mostly downhill route through the panoramic areas, Hypostyle Room, Nature Square, and dragon staircase. Allow two hours with sitting breaks.

Afternoon and evening

  • 11:45: Taxi or bus toward Gràcia.
  • 12:15–13:15: Casa Vicens if you want another Gaudí interior. Otherwise, see the exterior and save your energy for the neighborhood.
  • 13:30–15:00: Lunch around Plaça de la Virreina or Mercat de la Llibertat. La Pubilla is a popular lunch option; confirm its current hours.
  • 15:00–16:00: Gentle Gràcia stroll: Plaça de la Virreina, Plaça del Sol, and independent shops.
  • 16:00: Walk or taxi to the B&B; rest.
  • 19:30: Dinner close to the hotel.

Lower-energy version: Park Güell, taxi to lunch in Gràcia, then return to the B&B. Skip Casa Vicens.

Important: Park Güell is hilly even with the easier entrance. A taxi both ways is worthwhile.

Saturday, 10 October — Montjuïc views and the Mediterranean

This day combines two areas but includes a substantial hotel break.

Morning: Montjuïc

  • 09:15: Take L3 from Diagonal to Paral·lel, then the Montjuïc funicular. If the funicular is temporarily closed, TMB normally provides or identifies an alternative; check service notices that morning.
  • 10:00: Take the Montjuïc cable car for views and an easy ascent.
  • 10:15–11:30: Montjuïc Castle exterior, gardens, and viewpoints. Tour the interior only if military history interests you.
  • 11:30: Descend by cable car.
  • 12:00–13:30: Choose one cultural stop:
    • Fundació Joan Miró for modern art, or
    • Museu Nacional d’Art de Catalunya (MNAC) for Catalan art and the grand palace setting.
  • 13:45: Lunch in Poble-sec or near Plaça d’Espanya.
  • 15:00–17:00: Return to the B&B and rest.

Late afternoon: waterfront

  • 17:00: Taxi to Port Vell or Barceloneta. Stroll the marina and seafront promenade; this is for atmosphere, not beach weather.
  • 18:30: Early seafood or rice dinner. Can Solé is a classic option in Barceloneta; reserve and check current hours. Alternatively, eat around Port Vell/El Born for a broader choice.
  • 20:30: Taxi back to the B&B.

Lower-energy version: Spend the morning on Montjuïc, have lunch, and make the rest of the day free. The waterfront is optional.

Poor-weather version: Replace the cable car and waterfront with MNAC plus a relaxed afternoon in Eixample.

Sunday, 11 October — Departure for Hong Kong

Your confirmed arrival is 07:00 HKT on Monday 12 October. The published Cathay Pacific schedule for CX318 uses an approximately 13:00 CEST departure from Barcelona on the previous day for an arrival around 07:00 in Hong Kong. Verify the exact 11 October departure time and terminal on your Cathay Pacific e-ticket or in Manage Booking; if your ticket shows a different time, work backward from it using the same three-hour airport buffer.

  • 07:30: Breakfast at or near the B&B.
  • 08:15: Finish packing and check out. Ask the B&B the previous day to arrange a taxi large enough for you and your luggage.
  • 08:45–09:00: Leave the B&B by taxi for Barcelona–El Prat Airport.
  • Approximately 09:30–09:45: Arrive at the airport, about three hours before the expected long-haul departure. Go to the terminal shown by Cathay Pacific rather than relying on an older terminal listing.

Allow 30–45 minutes for the taxi, plus extra time for Sunday traffic or road disruption. There is not enough reliable time for La Pedrera or other ticketed sightseeing this morning.

Evening-before checklist: Complete online check-in if available, confirm the terminal and departure time, download boarding passes, organize passports, and settle the hotel bill if the B&B permits advance payment.

My recommendation

For a vegetarian-inclusive trip, make Rasoterra on Thursday your one guaranteed destination meal and use Bodega Joan and Can Solé for traditional local cooking with published vegetarian choices. Treat every Michelin reservation as conditional: contact the restaurant first and reserve only after it confirms a complete vegetarian experience.

  • If Lasarte confirms a full vegetarian menu, Wednesday is the most convenient slot and provides the grandest formal meal.
  • Choose Cinc Sentits or Mont Bar on Friday only after written confirmation that the kitchen can serve an equivalent vegetarian tasting sequence; their standard published menus are not dependable vegetarian choices.
  • Choose Koy Shunka only for non-vegetarian diners unless the restaurant explicitly confirms a complete menu without seafood, fish stock, dashi, or bonito.
  • Treat Disfrutar as a reservation-led itinerary change and verify vegetarian accommodation before securing the booking. If it cannot accommodate, do not reorganize the sightseeing day around it.

Booking advice

  • Reserve as soon as each restaurant releases October dates. Disfrutar may already be difficult; use its official waitlist rather than an unofficial reseller.
  • The Lasarte reservation page currently says it is closed Sunday, Monday, and Tuesday, making Wednesday 7 October your first possible evening. It requires a card guarantee and advance notice for changes or cancellations.
  • Expect tasting meals to last approximately 2.5–4 hours. For a late seating, be prepared to simplify or start the next morning more slowly if needed.
  • State allergies, intolerances, mobility needs, and foods you do not eat when booking, not after arrival. Complex tasting menus may not accommodate every restriction without notice.
  • Smart casual clothing is the safe choice; Lasarte is the dressiest of this shortlist. Comfortable but polished shoes are suitable.
  • Menus and prices change seasonally. Review the official booking page before confirming, including wine-pairing costs and cancellation terms.

Priority order for this vegetarian-inclusive trip: Rasoterra → Bodega Joan → Can Solé → any Michelin restaurant that explicitly confirms a full vegetarian menu.

Getting around

From the airport

  • Simplest: A licensed taxi directly to the B&B, particularly with luggage.
  • Lower-cost compromise: Aerobús to Plaça Universitat or Plaça Catalunya, then a short taxi to the B&B.
  • The airport metro is reliable but requires transfers and is less convenient with luggage for this address.

Within Barcelona

  • Your nearest useful station is Diagonal (L3/L5); Provença FGC is also nearby.
  • Use metro for Sagrada Família, the Old City, and Montjuïc. Use taxis for Park Güell and when tired.
  • For this walk-heavy itinerary, compare separate multi-journey cards with the unlimited Hola Barcelona Travel Card. The latter offers 2–5 consecutive days and includes eligible airport metro travel. Not every ordinary fare is valid at airport L9 stations; check the official TMB airport guidance and 2026 fares before purchase.
  • Each traveler should have the appropriate individual ticket or pass.

Comfort and practical notes

  • October conditions: Usually pleasant for walking, but rain and temperature swings are possible. Pack layers, a compact umbrella, and supportive shoes.
  • Meal rhythm: Lunch around 13:30–15:00 and dinner around 20:00 fits local schedules while avoiding the latest dining hours.
  • Rest: Wednesday and Saturday include intentional hotel breaks. Keep them; they will make the evenings more enjoyable.
  • Terrain: Park Güell and Montjuïc are the steepest days. Use taxis, funiculars, and cable cars rather than treating transport as wasted sightseeing time.
  • Safety: Barcelona is generally easy to navigate, but pickpocketing is common in crowded transport and tourist areas. Use a zipped cross-body bag, keep phones away from table edges, and ignore unsolicited “help” at ticket machines.
  • Water: Carry a small bottle and refill it. Sit for coffee whenever you need a break.
  • Sunday: Keep Sunday morning exclusively for checkout and the airport transfer; do not add a ticketed attraction.

Optional day-trip swap

If both of you strongly prefer mountain scenery and monasteries, replace Saturday’s Montjuïc/waterfront day with Montserrat. It requires a longer transit day, more planning, and some sloping terrain, so the main itinerary keeps all five sightseeing days in Barcelona. Do not sacrifice Sagrada Família, Park Güell, or the Old City for it on a first visit.
